# Session: 0.5.0 → 0.5.1 → ifcfast in the browser

## Summary
Two releases and a new product surface in one long session. v0.5.0 closed
the Layer-1 correctness programme; v0.5.1 shipped adaptive circle
tessellation with an area-preserving radius (#170), GUID-named glTF
materials (#146) and `by_source` on every mesh surface (#166), all
oracle-gated. Then, on Ed's decision "client-side only, no backend", the
core was compiled to WebAssembly (`crates/wasm`) and ifcfast.com's
instrument now takes a dropped IFC, streams its geometry batch by batch
into a three.js viewer, and cross-filters it — a 130 MB MEP model shows
first geometry in ~5 s at 60 fps and ~100 MB of heap. Ed also restated
the working rule mid-session: Fable plans and coordinates, opus/sonnet
agents write the code.

## Technical Details
The decisive findings are in the per-topic worklogs. The three that
would be expensive to rediscover: inscribed circle sampling cannot meet
the ±0.005 oracle band below n = 24 (hence the area-preserving radius);
`graphJson()` before `streamMeshes()` eagerly builds the whole graph
(6.6–8.8 s on RIV); and the instrument grid must never require data
that only exists at "done" (it unmounted for the entire stream —
nothing was ever streaming on screen before `e6caabf`).
